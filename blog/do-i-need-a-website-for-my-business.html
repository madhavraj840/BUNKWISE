Do I Need a Website for My Small Business? (Or Is Instagram Enough?)

Instagram, Google profile or a website?

Only one of them is fully yours.

Websiteyou control it Instagramrented reach Googlelocal discovery

Quick answer

Most small businesses benefit from a website, because it is the one place online that you fully control. Instagram and a Google Business Profile are useful, but the platforms decide who sees them. If customers search for what you sell, compare options, or want details such as prices, menus or services before contacting you, a website usually pays for itself. A very small business that runs on word of mouth can start with a Google Business Profile and add a website later.

What a website does that social media can't

  • You own it. Social platforms change their rules and algorithms often. Your website stays yours.
  • It shows up in search. People searching Google for a service near them are more likely to find a website page than an Instagram post.
  • AI assistants can read it. Tools like ChatGPT, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews rely on clear, text-based information. A well-structured website is one of their best sources about your business.
  • It builds trust. Many customers check for a website before calling, especially for higher-value services.
  • It answers questions for you. Prices, menus, timings and services are available at 2 a.m. without anyone picking up the phone.

Website vs Instagram vs Google Business Profile

These are not competitors. Most successful small businesses use all three, with each pointing customers to the others.

When you can wait

A website is not urgent for every business. You can reasonably wait if:

  • nearly all your customers come from word of mouth or repeat business,
  • nobody searches online for what you offer in your area, or
  • you are already fully booked and can't take more enquiries.

In that case, set up a complete Google Business Profile first. It is free and covers the basics.

Signs you need a website now

  • Customers ask for your website link, and you have nothing to send.
  • Competitors appear on Google for the services you offer, and you don't.
  • You answer the same questions on the phone every day: prices, timings, menus, location.
  • You want customers from other cities or countries.
  • You are spending money on ads, with nowhere good to send the clicks.

What your first website should include

Start small and clear: a Home page that says what you do and where, an About page, a Services (or Menu/Products) page, and a Contact page with a WhatsApp or call button and a map. Our page-by-page checklist covers the details.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Google Business Profile enough without a website?

For a small local business that is found mostly on Google Maps, a well-kept Google Business Profile can be enough to start. It becomes limiting once customers want details before contacting you, or when you want to appear for searches beyond Maps. The profile works best when it links to a website.

Can I build a website myself?

Yes. Website builders make a simple site possible without code. The trade-off is your time, and it is harder to get strong design, speed and SEO without experience. Many owners start with a builder and move to a professional site once the business depends on online enquiries.

How long does it take to get a small business website?

A simple 3–4 page website can be ready in about a week, and most small business websites take 1 to 6 weeks depending on the number of pages and how quickly the content and photos are ready.
