Quick answer

In 2026, a professional small business website in the US typically costs $2,000–$8,000 from a freelancer and $10,000–$35,000 from an agency, according to Elementor's 2026 pricing breakdown. A do-it-yourself builder costs about $15–$50 a month. After launch, budget roughly $50–$200 a month for maintenance on top of hosting. BunkWise's packages for US businesses start at $999 as a one-time price.

American website quotes vary more than almost anywhere else in the world. A plumber in Ohio can be quoted $600 by a one-person shop and $25,000 by a marketing agency for what looks like the same five pages. The gap is not random: it comes from who does the work, how much of it is custom, and how much of the job is really strategy, writing and search optimisation rather than design. This guide breaks the US market down using figures published in 2026, so you can tell which kind of quote you are looking at.

What moves a US quote up or down

Two quotes for "a website" can differ ten times over because they describe different jobs. When you compare, look at these line items rather than the total:

  • Page count and page types. A four-page site is a different job from a site with separate pages for every service and every town you cover. Service-area pages are also what help a local business show up in more searches.
  • Custom design or theme. A theme-based site is quicker and cheaper. A custom layout costs more but fits your brand and the way your customers actually decide.
  • Copywriting. GoDaddy prices professional copy at around $0.35 a word, and Elementor puts blog posts at $100–$300 each. If you write the text yourself, the price drops, but so does the chance the pages are written around what people search.
  • What "SEO included" means. It can mean anything from page titles to full keyword research, structured data and local search setup. WebFX prices SEO work within a build at $2,000–$10,000 on its own.
  • Features. Online booking, payments, product catalogues and integrations with your CRM all add build time.

What US web designers charge per hour

If a provider quotes by the hour, the US range is wide. Elementor puts experienced freelancers at $75–$150 an hour. For comparison, the US Bureau of Labor Statistics reports a median wage of $47.85 an hour ($99,520 a year) for employed web developers and digital designers in 2025. The difference is not profit alone: a freelancer or agency pays for software, insurance, unbilled sales time and quiet months out of that hourly rate.

Hourly billing is common for changes after launch. For the build itself, most small businesses are better served by a fixed-price quote with a written list of pages and features, so the risk of overruns sits with the provider rather than with you.

What is different about buying a website in the US

A few things are specific to the American market and are worth raising with any provider before you sign.

Accessibility lawsuits are common

Website accessibility is a live legal risk in the US in a way it is not in most countries. Law firm Seyfarth Shaw counted 3,117 federal website accessibility lawsuits in 2025, 27% more than the 2,452 filed in 2024. New York (1,021), Florida (961) and Illinois (585) saw the most federal filings. EcomBack, which also counts state-court cases, recorded 3,948 lawsuits in 2025, with California third behind New York and Florida.

Small businesses are sued too, not only large retailers. Ask any designer which accessibility checks they run: image descriptions, colour contrast, keyboard navigation, labelled form fields and readable headings are basics that cost little when built in from the start.

Sales tax on web design depends on your state

In most states, custom website design is treated as a non-taxable service, but not everywhere. Washington began charging retail sales tax on website development from October 1, 2025, and the Pennsylvania Department of Revenue treats website design as taxable when the site is transferred to the customer. If you are in a state that taxes digital services, ask whether the quote includes tax.

Local listings matter as much as the site

For a local business, the website works together with its Google Business Profile, Yelp page and Apple Maps listing. A good build links them, uses the same business name, address and phone number everywhere, and adds LocalBusiness structured data so search engines and AI assistants can match the site to the listings.

The running costs after launch

The build is a one-time cost. A US website also has yearly and monthly costs that are easy to forget when comparing quotes:

  • Domain name: $0–$30 a year for a standard .com (GoDaddy).
  • Hosting: from about $6 a month for shared hosting to $300 or more for high-traffic sites (GoDaddy); managed WordPress hosting is typically $30–$75 a month (Elementor).
  • Maintenance plan: $50–$200 a month for updates, backups and small edits, plus $10–$30 a month for a security firewall (Elementor).
  • Ongoing SEO: optional, but it is what keeps a site climbing after launch.

A cheap build that leaves out content, SEO or support often ends up costing more once you add those back in. Compare the total for the first two years, not just the launch price.

How to compare US website quotes

  1. Ask for the exact list of pages and features, in writing.
  2. Check that the domain and hosting account will be registered in your name, not the designer's.
  3. Ask what "SEO" includes: keyword research, page titles, structured data, Google Search Console and Business Profile setup.
  4. Ask which accessibility checks they run before launch.
  5. Ask who writes the text and who supplies the photos.
  6. Ask what changes cost after launch, and how quickly they are made.
  7. Open two or three live sites they built for businesses like yours on your phone, and see how fast they load.

Hiring outside the US: an honest look

Many US businesses now hire designers abroad, mainly to get more work for the same budget. It works well when both sides plan around the differences:

  • Time zones. India is 9.5 hours ahead of New York in summer and 10.5 hours in winter, and 12.5–13.5 hours ahead of Los Angeles. In practice, you send feedback at the end of your day and wake up to the changes. Live calls work best early in the US morning.
  • Communication. Most of the project runs in writing, over email, WhatsApp or Telegram, with a short video call at each milestone.
  • Ownership. Your contract should state that you own the finished site, and the domain and hosting should be in your name from day one.
  • What you give up. There are no in-person meetings, and a designer abroad will not know your town the way a local one does, so you will need to share more about your customers and competitors.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a small business website cost in the US?

Most US small businesses pay $2,000–$8,000 for a 5–10 page site built by a freelancer, or $10,000–$35,000 through an agency, according to Elementor's 2026 breakdown. A DIY builder costs roughly $15–$50 a month instead.

How much do US web designers charge per hour?

Freelance web designers in the US commonly bill $75–$150 an hour. For context, the Bureau of Labor Statistics puts the median employee wage for web developers and digital designers at $47.85 an hour; freelancers and agencies charge more because they carry their own overheads.

Do I have to pay sales tax on web design in the US?

In most states custom web design is not taxed, but there are exceptions. Washington began taxing website development on October 1, 2025, and Pennsylvania taxes website design when the site is transferred to the customer. Check your own state's rules.

Does my small business website have to be ADA compliant?

US courts have repeatedly treated inaccessible websites as a disability-access issue, and 3,117 website accessibility lawsuits were filed in federal court in 2025, according to Seyfarth Shaw. Building to WCAG accessibility guidelines from the start is far cheaper than fixing a site after a demand letter.

Is it safe to hire a web designer outside the US?

It can be, if you check live websites they have built, get a written scope with fixed prices, pay in milestones and keep your domain and hosting in your own name. The main trade-off is the time difference: India is 9.5–10.5 hours ahead of New York.
