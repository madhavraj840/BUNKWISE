Quick answer

In 2026, a professional custom website in Charlotte typically costs $3,500–$6,500, with basic starter sites at $1,500–$3,000, according to Charlotte Web Design Studio. In Raleigh and the Triangle, lean studios sell sites from $399–$1,699 (Anchor Web Digital), while agencies quote $6,000–$15,000+ and full agency projects run up to $75,000+ (Vaulted). North Carolina has two distinct web design markets sitting side by side. At one end are lean studios and freelancers selling small business websites for a few hundred to a couple of thousand dollars; at the other are Charlotte and Research Triangle agencies building strategy-led sites for tech, healthcare and professional-services firms. Both are legitimate, and they are selling different things. This guide shows where each sits, then covers a North Carolina tax rule that affects how website projects should be invoiced.

The studio's summary is that a realistic investment for a well-built Charlotte small business site is usually $3,500–$6,500, with simpler projects lower and more advanced custom work higher. Where a five-page site lands inside the $2,500–$5,500 band comes down to the quality of the design, how much content is needed and the amount of SEO setup.

Raleigh and the Triangle: two ends of the market

Anchor Web Digital, a Raleigh studio, argues that most Raleigh small businesses can get a professional site for $399–$1,699. In its breakdown, agencies across the Triangle start around $6,000 and run past $15,000, template sites or freelancers cost $600–$2,000, and custom small business sites from lean studios span $500–$5,000. A DIY builder costs up to about $40 a month, plus the hours you put in yourself.

Vaulted, a Raleigh agency, describes the other end. It puts professional agency web design at $9,000–$75,000+ depending on scope, with projects that need wireframes, prototypes, responsive mockups and original branding typically at $25,000–$75,000. A HubSpot CMS build with CRM integration, marketing automation or e-commerce adds another $5,000–$20,000 or more, and having the agency write the content usually adds $2,000–$10,000, with page count driving the total.

Both views can be true at once. Anchor Web Digital points out that many Triangle customers work in technology, healthcare or professional services and check a business online before getting in touch, which raises the bar for credibility and speed even on a small site. A plumber in Cary and a software company in Durham still need very different websites. The useful question is which of them your business resembles.

North Carolina's digital property tax and bundled invoices

North Carolina does not generally tax professional services, but it does tax certain digital property delivered electronically, including photographs, digital audiovisual works and publications. That matters for website projects, which often include exactly those deliverables: product photos, videos, downloadable guides.

In September 2026, the Department of Revenue published a ruling (SUPLR 2026-0004) about a remote contractor who supplied marketing and graphic-design services and delivered digital files by email and cloud links. Because the nontaxable design services were not separately stated from the taxable digital property, the whole charge was treated as taxable, as VATupdate's summary explains. The lesson for any North Carolina business buying a website: ask for design and development to be priced separately from any photography, video or other digital products in the contract and on the invoice.

Privacy and accessibility

North Carolina has no comprehensive consumer privacy law in force, according to the IAPP's state tracker. Federal accessibility law still applies, so building to WCAG guidelines is sensible for any business that serves the public, especially one with physical premises.

Running costs after launch

  • Hosting and maintenance: ask for monthly care to be quoted separately; national figures from Elementor put maintenance plans at $50–$200 a month.
  • DIY platforms: up to about $40 a month (Anchor Web Digital).
  • Content: $2,000–$10,000 if an agency writes the site (Vaulted).
  • CRM and automation: $5,000–$20,000+ extra for HubSpot-style integrations, if you need them.

How to compare North Carolina quotes

  1. Decide whether you need a lean small business site or a strategy-led agency project, and compare like with like.
  2. Ask for design and development to be itemised separately from photography, video and other digital deliverables.
  3. Ask who writes the content and whether SEO setup is included.
  4. Check whether a low price includes hosting, revisions and support.
  5. Confirm the domain and site will be owned by your business.
  6. Look at live sites the provider has built for similar businesses.

Hiring outside North Carolina

North Carolina is on Eastern Time, so India is 9.5 hours ahead in summer and 10.5 in winter. With a designer in India, a Charlotte or Raleigh business can send notes in the afternoon and review changes the next morning, with calls early in the day.

The itemised-invoice point applies to any provider, local or overseas. Beyond that, check live work, agree a written scope and milestone payments, and keep your accounts in your own name. Our guide to hiring a web designer from India covers these in detail.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a small business website cost in Charlotte?

Charlotte Web Design Studio puts a basic starter site at $1,500–$3,000 and a professional custom website at $3,500–$6,500, and says a five-page site usually costs $2,500–$5,500, with design, content and SEO setup deciding where in that range it falls.

How much does a website cost in Raleigh?

It depends heavily on who you hire. Anchor Web Digital says many Raleigh small businesses pay $399–$1,699 through lean studios, while Triangle agencies quote $6,000–$15,000+, and Vaulted puts full agency projects at $9,000–$75,000+.

Is web design taxed in North Carolina?

Professional services are generally not taxed, but North Carolina taxes certain digital property delivered electronically, such as photographs, audiovisual works and publications. In a 2026 ruling, the Department of Revenue taxed an entire bundled charge because nontaxable design services weren't separately stated from taxable digital deliverables.

How much does website copywriting add in North Carolina?

Vaulted, a Raleigh agency, says having an agency handle content and copywriting usually adds $2,000–$10,000, with the number of pages driving the figure.

Do I need a big agency for a small business website in North Carolina?

Usually not. For a single-location service business, a custom 5–10 page site from a studio or experienced freelancer covers what most need. Large agency projects are aimed at custom UX research, CRM integration and marketing automation.
