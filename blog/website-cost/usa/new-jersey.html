Quick answer

In 2026, most New Jersey small businesses spend $500–$6,500 on their initial website plus $50–$200 a month to run it, according to PixelVerse Studios. A 5–8 page site built to generate leads typically costs $2,000–$4,000, and local custom builds $3,000–$15,000 (Jersey Circle). Website design and hosting are exempt from New Jersey sales tax, but the state's privacy law can apply to smaller businesses than similar laws elsewhere. BunkWise's packages start at $999.

New Jersey businesses sit next to one of the most expensive web design markets in the world, but most don't need to pay Manhattan prices. Local studios and freelancers across the state build small business websites for a fraction of New York agency rates. Two things are specific to New Jersey: web design is explicitly exempt from sales tax, and the state's 2025 privacy law has no revenue threshold, so it can reach small businesses with large customer lists.

The Jersey Shore and the New York shadow

Jersey Circle, which works with businesses in Brick, Toms River, Red Bank and Asbury Park, gives a view from Monmouth and Ocean County:

  • DIY or template: $0–$1,500
  • Freelance or overseas: $1,500–$3,000
  • Local custom: $3,000–$15,000
  • Custom or enterprise: $15,000–$50,000+

North Jersey businesses are more likely to be quoted by New York agencies. NixMar's 2026 guide to the tri-state market puts 15–30 page professional or corporate sites from suburban Connecticut and New Jersey agencies at $12,000–$30,000, against $25,000–$60,000 from NYC mid-tier agencies. For a local service business, the $2,000–$8,000 tiers above are usually the relevant comparison, not the New York agency market.

Template platform or custom build?

PixelVerse makes a point about total cost that is easy to miss. A builder advertised at around $200 a year often ends up at $500–$1,000 a year once apps, ad removal and a custom domain are added, so after three years you may have spent $1,500–$3,000 on a platform you don't own. A custom-coded site costs more upfront but can run on cheap hosting afterwards. Its own worked example, a $2,000 build plus $79 a month, comes to about $2,950 in the first year. Compare options over three years, not one.

New Jersey's privacy law and tax position

The New Jersey Data Privacy Act

The NJDPA took effect on January 15, 2025. According to employment law firm Ogletree, it applies to businesses that control or process the personal data of 100,000 or more New Jersey consumers, or 25,000 or more if they earn revenue from selling personal data. Unlike California's law, it has no revenue threshold. A small retailer or clinic with a large mailing list could therefore be covered, and would need a compliant privacy notice and a way for people to exercise their rights. If your customer base is in that range, raise it with your designer and a lawyer.

Web design and hosting are tax-exempt

The New Jersey Division of Taxation's Sales Tax Guide (S&U-4) lists both website design and website hosting as exempt. The same guide lists specified digital products, such as digital music, e-books and other downloads, as taxable, so if a project bundles digital products with design work, ask for them to be invoiced separately.

Running costs after launch

  • Hosting, maintenance and updates: $50–$200 a month for most small businesses (PixelVerse); maintenance retainers alone typically $50–$300.
  • Custom-coded sites: hosting typically $10–$50 a month.
  • Static hosting: Jersey Circle notes a static site with email forwarding can run for $0–$50 a month, while managed WordPress with premium plugins can pass $200.
  • DIY platforms: a "$200 a year" builder often becomes $500–$1,000 a year once apps, ad removal and a custom domain are added (PixelVerse).

How to compare New Jersey quotes

  1. Check that no sales tax has been added to design or hosting, which are exempt.
  2. If you hold data on a large number of NJ customers, ask how the site will handle privacy notices and requests.
  3. Get a written page list and fixed price, and compare tiers like for like.
  4. Ask what hosting setup is proposed and what it will cost each month.
  5. Confirm the domain and site will be owned by your business.
  6. Look at live sites the provider has built for businesses like yours.

Hiring outside New Jersey

New Jersey is on Eastern Time, so India is 9.5 hours ahead in summer and 10.5 in winter. Working with a designer there, the practical rhythm is written feedback during your day, changes made overnight, and a short call early in your morning. Jersey Circle lists freelance and overseas builds at $1,500–$3,000, a reminder that "overseas" covers a wide range of quality; live work and a written scope are what separate them.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a small business website cost in New Jersey?

Most New Jersey small businesses spend $500–$6,500 on their initial website, plus $50–$200 a month for hosting, maintenance and updates, according to PixelVerse Studios. A 5–8 page site built for leads typically costs $2,000–$4,000.

Is website design taxable in New Jersey?

No. The New Jersey Division of Taxation's Sales Tax Guide lists website design and website hosting as exempt from sales tax. Specified digital products, such as downloadable music or e-books, are taxable, so keep those separate on an invoice.

Does the New Jersey Data Privacy Act apply to small businesses?

It can. The NJDPA took effect on January 15, 2025 and applies to businesses that process the personal data of 100,000 or more New Jersey consumers, or 25,000 or more if they earn revenue from selling personal data. It has no revenue threshold, so a small business with a large customer list can be covered.

How much does a website cost in Monmouth or Ocean County?

Jersey Circle, a local studio, puts DIY or template sites at $0–$1,500, freelance or overseas builds at $1,500–$3,000, local custom sites at $3,000–$15,000, and custom or enterprise builds at $15,000–$50,000+.

Are New Jersey web designers cheaper than New York City?

Generally yes. NixMar's 2026 guide to the tri-state market puts 15–30 page professional or corporate sites from suburban New Jersey and Connecticut agencies at $12,000–$30,000, against $25,000–$60,000 from NYC mid-tier agencies.
