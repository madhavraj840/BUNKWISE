Quick answer

In 2026, a custom, SEO-ready small business website in Chicago typically costs $2,500–$5,000, and a fully custom site with copywriting and strategy $5,000–$10,000 or more, according to Seven Seas Web Design. Template sites cost $500–$2,000. Across the market, Windy City Web Designs puts freelancers at $1,500–$8,000+ and web design companies at $3,000–$15,000+. Prices by provider

Windy City Web Designs looks at the Illinois market by provider in its August 2026 guide. Hiring a freelancer typically costs between $1,500 and $8,000 or more, and a web design firm from $3,000 to over $15,000. A do-it-yourself site can cost almost nothing or run past $1,000 with paid themes and apps, while complex custom projects start around $10,000 and can exceed $30,000.

Both guides, written by Chicago-area agencies, make the same point from different directions: a $500 site that never brings in business isn't cheaper than a $5,000 site that produces leads for years, but most small businesses also don't need to spend $20,000 to look professional.

Illinois specifics: tax, lawsuits and monthly plans

No sales tax on web design

Illinois does not tax sales of services. The Illinois Department of Revenue explains that its sales taxes apply to tangible personal property, including property transferred as part of a service, rather than to the service itself. Designing and building a website is a service, so a quote from an Illinois provider shouldn't include sales tax on the design work. Physical items or prewritten software licences sold alongside can be treated differently, and the Department notes that targeted advertising and digital assets become taxable from January 1, 2027, so ask how any ad management or bundled digital products in a quote will be taxed.

Third in the country for accessibility lawsuits

Illinois had 585 federal website accessibility lawsuits in 2025, the third-highest total behind New York and Florida, according to law firm Seyfarth Shaw. For a Chicago business with a storefront, office or restaurant, accessibility belongs in the brief: descriptive image text, adequate colour contrast, keyboard-friendly menus and properly labelled forms.

Monthly website plans

Some Chicago providers offer websites on a subscription. Windy City Web Designs sells a $300-a-month WordPress plan that includes design, management and updates, and it does the arithmetic openly: $7,200 over two years. A plan can make sense if you want ongoing help and predictable costs; buying outright is usually cheaper if the site needs few changes. Either way, ask what happens to the site and domain if you cancel.

Running costs after launch

Seven Seas lists the running costs a Chicago small business should plan for:

  • Hosting: $5–$50 a month; very cheap shared hosting can slow the site and hurt rankings.
  • Domain: $12–$20 a year for a .com.
  • Maintenance plan: $50–$200 a month for a managed WordPress site.
  • Changes after launch: $75–$150 an hour from a freelancer.
  • Premium plugins: $50–$400 a year each for booking, payments, forms or security.
  • Photography and copywriting: $400–$2,000 for a shoot and $500–$2,000 for professional copy.

How to compare Illinois quotes

  1. Match each quote to the budget table above: which outcome is it actually designed for?
  2. Ask whether copywriting and photography are included.
  3. For monthly plans, total the cost over two or three years and check exit terms.
  4. Ask which accessibility checks will be run before launch.
  5. Confirm you will own the domain and the finished site.
  6. Ask what changes cost after launch, and whether a maintenance plan is optional.

Hiring outside Illinois

Frequently asked questions

How much does a small business website cost in Chicago?

Seven Seas Web Design puts a custom, SEO-ready Chicago website at $2,500–$5,000 and a fully custom site with copywriting and strategy at $5,000–$10,000 or more. Windy City Web Designs gives $1,500–$8,000+ for a freelancer and $3,000–$15,000+ for a web design company.

Is web design taxed in Illinois?

No. Illinois does not tax sales of services, according to the Illinois Department of Revenue, and creating a website is a service. Tangible goods sold alongside a project can be taxable.

Should I pay monthly for a website instead of buying one?

It depends on how long you keep it and what the plan includes. Windy City Web Designs, which sells a $300-a-month plan, points out that this totals $7,200 over two years. Monthly plans bundle management and updates; buying outright usually costs less over several years if you need few changes.

Are there website accessibility lawsuits in Illinois?

Yes. Illinois had 585 federal website accessibility lawsuits in 2025, the third-highest of any state, according to law firm Seyfarth Shaw.

How much do Chicago web designers charge for changes after launch?

Seven Seas Web Design says freelancers typically bill $75–$150 an hour for changes after launch, so a two-hour homepage update costs $150–$300. A managed maintenance plan typically costs $50–$200 a month.
