Florida's website prices span a very wide range, from $279 to $75,000 or more in Tamer Marketing's Miami guide. Miami in particular has an active, loudly advertised budget tier, a crowded middle of independent designers and small studios, and agencies charging tens of thousands for growth and e-commerce projects. On top of that, Florida is one of the states where website accessibility lawsuits are most common, which makes build quality a legal question as well as a marketing one.

What the budget tier really buys

Tamer Marketing is refreshingly direct about the bottom of the market: at the $279 end, the owner typically drops their own words into a ready-made demo; by $1,500, the provider loads the content, configures email and hands over a finished site. The honest providers deliver exactly what they promise, but the promise is small.

Florida also has fixed-price packages aimed at the gap between templates and agencies. Search Scale AI, for example, lists a 5–7 page site at $697, a 10–15 page site with a blog and call tracking at $1,097, and a 20+ page site at $1,597. Packages like these can be good value, but compare them line by line: number of revision rounds, who writes the copy, whether hosting is bundled, and who owns the site if you leave. Search Scale AI notes that Florida agencies commonly charge $75–$150 a page when they write the copy, which can add $1,000–$2,000 to a 15-page project.

Accessibility lawsuits and other Florida specifics

Florida is a hotspot for ADA website claims

Law firm Seyfarth Shaw counted 961 federal website accessibility lawsuits in Florida in 2025, second only to New York's 1,021, out of 3,117 nationally. For a Florida restaurant, clinic, hotel or shop, that is a strong argument for building to WCAG accessibility guidelines from the start: proper image descriptions, readable colour contrast, keyboard-accessible menus and forms with visible labels. Ask any provider, budget or premium, what they check before launch.

Sales tax: services are mostly outside it

Florida uses an enumerated-services approach: as Reed Corporation, a Miami CPA firm, puts it, if a service isn't on the statutory list, it isn't subject to Florida sales tax. Custom design and development delivered digitally generally falls outside that list. Tangible items bundled into a project can change the picture, so keep them separately invoiced.

Privacy law is aimed at the very largest companies

Florida's privacy law is much narrower than California's, and MultiState's 2026 review of state privacy laws notes its limited scope. For a typical small business, what matters is a clear privacy policy and careful handling of the details people submit through forms.

Running costs after launch

  • Managed hosting: $10–$50 a month for quality hosting (Search Scale AI).
  • Maintenance retainer: $100–$300 a month for updates and security.
  • Running costs for a small site: $75–$200 a month in Tamer Marketing's worked example.
  • Payment processing: roughly 2.9% plus $0.30 per transaction if you sell online. By Tamer's arithmetic, a shop taking $200,000 a year pays roughly $6,100 in fees annually, which outweighs most of the build choices owners worry about.

How to compare Florida quotes

  1. Ask which accessibility checks the provider runs, and get WCAG named in the contract.
  2. For fixed packages, list what is included: pages, copy, revisions, hosting, ownership.
  3. Get timelines in writing; Florida's budget tier promises days, larger sites take weeks.
  4. Confirm the domain and hosting are registered to your business.
  5. If you sell online, compare payment processing fees as carefully as build prices.
  6. Look at the three-year total, not just the launch price.

Hiring outside Florida

Most of Florida is on Eastern Time, so India is 9.5 hours ahead in summer and 10.5 in winter; the Panhandle, on Central Time, is an hour further back. A working rhythm that suits both sides is written feedback during your day and a short call early in your morning, which is the Indian evening.

Given Florida's lawsuit numbers, the most important thing to agree with any designer, local or overseas, is the accessibility standard. Frequently asked questions

How much does a small business website cost in Florida?

A 5–10 page small business site in Miami typically costs $2,500–$6,000, and a larger, search-focused growth site $6,000–$15,000, according to Tamer Marketing's 2026 guide. Budget flat-fee packages start around $279 but usually mean a template with your own content.

Are ADA website lawsuits common in Florida?

Yes. Florida had 961 federal website accessibility lawsuits in 2025, second only to New York, according to law firm Seyfarth Shaw. Businesses with a physical location open to the public are the usual targets, so building to accessibility guidelines is worth the small extra effort.

Is website design taxed in Florida?

Florida taxes services only if they appear on a statutory list, and custom design and development delivered digitally generally falls outside it. Taxable tangible items delivered with a project can change that, so ask your accountant if a quote bundles products with services.

What does a website cost to run in Florida after launch?

Search Scale AI puts quality managed hosting at $10–$50 a month and maintenance retainers at $100–$300 a month. If you sell online, Tamer Marketing points to card processing fees of about 2.9% plus 30 cents on every sale.

Do Florida web designers offer cheap fixed-price packages?

Yes. Several Florida providers sell fixed packages, for example Search Scale AI's range from $697 for 5–7 pages to $1,597 for 20+ pages. Check what each package includes, especially copywriting, revisions and who owns the site.
