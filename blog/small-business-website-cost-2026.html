Quick answer

In 2026, a professional small business website typically costs $2,000–$8,000 in the US, £3,000–£6,000 in the UK and ₹14,999–₹49,999 for an agency package in India. Basic template sites cost far less, and custom builds with online payments or large catalogues cost more. The price mostly depends on the number of pages, how custom the design is, and whether SEO and content writing are included.

What actually drives the price

Two quotes for "a website" can differ ten times over because they describe different things. These are the factors that move the number most:

  • Number of pages. A 4-page site (Home, About, Services, Contact) is quicker to build than a 15-page site with separate service and location pages.
  • Custom design or template. Templates are cheaper but shared by thousands of sites. Custom design takes longer and costs more, but fits your brand and your customers.
  • Who writes the content. If you supply text and photos, the price drops. Professional copywriting that targets search keywords adds cost but usually improves results.
  • SEO depth. "SEO included" can mean anything from page titles to full keyword research, schema markup and local SEO. Ask exactly what is included.
  • Features. Online booking, payments, product catalogues, multiple languages and integrations all add time.
  • Who builds it. A website builder, a freelancer and an agency each price differently. See our comparison of website builders, freelancers and agencies.

The costs people forget to budget for

The build is a one-time cost, but a website also has running costs:

  • Domain name and hosting, which renew every year.
  • Updates and changes, such as new photos, prices or services.
  • Ongoing SEO, if you want to keep climbing in Google. Local SEO for a small business typically costs $500–$3,000 a month in the US, according to WebFX; it costs considerably less in India.

A cheap quote that leaves out content, SEO or post-launch support can end up costing more once you add those back in.

How to compare two website quotes

Before you choose, ask every provider the same questions so you compare like with like:

  1. How many pages are included, and which ones?
  2. Is the design custom, or based on a template?
  3. Who writes the text, and is it written for search keywords?
  4. What exactly does "SEO" include? Keyword research, schema, Google Search Console setup?
  5. How fast does the site load on a phone?
  6. What happens after launch, and what do changes cost?
  7. Can I see live websites you have built for businesses like mine?

Is a cheaper website a false economy?

It depends on what the website needs to do. If you only need an online business card with your phone number and address, a basic site or a website builder can be enough. If the website is meant to bring in customers from Google, the difference between a basic and a well-researched site shows up in rankings and enquiries over the following months. In that case, a mid-range package is usually better value than the cheapest one.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a professional website for under ₹10,000?

Yes. In India, budget designers advertise small sites from around ₹3,800–₹7,800, and starter packages from studios such as BunkWise begin at ₹9,999 + GST. At this level, expect 3–5 pages, a mobile-friendly design and basic SEO rather than custom features or content writing.

How much should a small business spend on a website?

Enough for the website to realistically pay for itself. If it only needs to show your contact details, a basic site is fine. If it needs to bring in enquiries from Google, budget for custom design, keyword research and on-page SEO, which usually means a mid-range package rather than the cheapest option.

Are there monthly costs after a website is built?

Usually yes. Expect yearly renewals for the domain name and hosting, occasional updates, and optionally a monthly SEO plan if you want to keep improving your Google rankings.
