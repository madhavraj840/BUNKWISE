Quick answer

In 2026, most New York local businesses that want a website that brings in customers spend $3,000–$10,000, according to Wintra. Freelancers charge $1,000–$5,000 and small agencies $3,000–$15,000. At the top end, a 10-page custom site from a Manhattan agency runs $30,000–$60,000, while upstate, a basic site costs $1,000–$3,000. Website development isn't subject to New York sales tax. "New York" covers two very different web design markets. In Manhattan and Brooklyn, agencies price for national brands and funded start-ups; upstate, in cities like Syracuse, Rochester or Albany, a small business is more likely to work with a freelancer or a two-person studio at a fraction of the price. The work involved in a good five-page site is much the same in both. This guide gives the ranges for each, then the New York rules that affect any website: the country's highest volume of accessibility lawsuits and a favourable sales tax position.

Wintra offers a useful sanity check for quotes: honest custom work on a small business site involves roughly 40–80 hours of design, build, writing and testing. A quote of $2,000 for all of that means either very low rates or, more likely, parts of the job quietly left out. It also flags copywriting and photography as the hidden difference between a $2,000 and a $6,000 quote: "you provide the text" is a big line item in disguise.

Manhattan versus upstate

The price gap inside the state is large. NixMar's 2026 guide to the New York and Connecticut market, citing Allusive Digital's figures, puts a 10-page custom site from a Manhattan agency at $30,000–$60,000, with senior hourly rates of $150–$250 and premium boutiques at $200–$400. For a 5–10 page small business site, the same guide puts Manhattan boutiques at $8,000–$25,000. It describes the tri-state corridor as sitting 20–60% above the national average.

Upstate looks very different. NRS Virtual Services, writing about Central New York in August 2026, gives:

  • Basic website: $1,000–$3,000
  • Mid-level website: $3,000–$7,000
  • Advanced website: $7,000–$15,000+
  • Maintenance: $500–$2,000 a year

For a restaurant in Queens or a contractor in Buffalo, there is rarely a reason to pay Manhattan agency rates. What matters is the scope of the work, and that can be compared across both markets.

Lawsuits, tax and other New York specifics

The most accessibility lawsuits in the country

New York led the country in 2025 with 1,021 federal website accessibility lawsuits, according to law firm Seyfarth Shaw, a third of the 3,117 filed nationally. Building to WCAG accessibility guidelines costs little when it is part of the original plan: alt text for images, enough colour contrast, keyboard-friendly navigation, labelled form fields and captions for video.

Web design isn't taxed

New York only taxes services that are specifically listed in its Tax Law, and website development is not one of them. The Department of Taxation and Finance confirmed in advisory opinion TSB-A-20(70)S that website development services, including consulting, design and creation, are not subject to sales and use tax, a position it has held in earlier opinions going back to the 1990s.

Own your domain

Wintra makes a point that deserves emphasis in a market with this many providers: pay for your domain and hosting directly, in your business's name. A $15 domain registered in an agency's name can leave a business stuck if the relationship ends. Ask for renewal prices after year one too, since introductory hosting rates often rise.

Running costs after launch

  • Domain: $12–$20 a year, registered in your name (Wintra).
  • Hosting: $0–$50 a month; a well-built static site can run on nearly free hosting, while managed WordPress typically costs $25–$50.
  • Maintenance: $50–$200 a month if you outsource updates, backups and security.
  • Business email: $6–$12 per user a month.
  • SSL: free on any modern platform; Wintra treats a separate "SSL fee" as a red flag.

How to compare New York quotes

  1. Send every provider the same written scope, so the quotes describe the same job.
  2. Ask for copywriting, photography and SEO to be priced as separate lines.
  3. Ask which accessibility checks are run, and name WCAG in the contract.
  4. Register the domain and hosting yourself.
  5. Check live sites the provider has built, on your phone, and test their speed.
  6. Compare the two-year total, including maintenance.

Hiring outside New York

India is 9.5 hours ahead of New York in summer and 10.5 in winter. With a designer there, the useful overlap is early in the New York morning; the rest of the project runs in writing, with changes made overnight. For a busy owner, that can be easier than finding meeting slots during the week.

The checks that matter are the same as for any provider: an accessibility standard in the contract, live work you can open, a written scope and milestone payments. Our guide to hiring a web designer from India goes through them.
