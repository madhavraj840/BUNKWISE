California is one of the most expensive web design markets in the US, and also one where a website carries the most legal weight. The state's civil rights law makes accessibility failures costly for businesses with a physical location, and its privacy law is the strictest in the country for larger companies. This guide covers what Californian freelancers and agencies charge in 2026, then the rules that make a California website different from one built for Ohio or Georgia.

Los Angeles prices by type of site

Brainbox, an agency writing about the Los Angeles market in September 2026, gives planning ranges rather than fixed prices. For most small businesses the relevant figure is $3,000–$10,000 for a complete business website. A one-page site for a campaign or launch comes in at $1,500–$4,000, while template sites start from about $300. At the upper end, Brainbox places custom marketing sites from $7,500 to $20,000 or more, online shops from $5,000 to over $30,000, and corporate websites from $10,000 to more than $40,000.

Brainbox is candid that these are planning ranges rather than fixed prices, and notes that at the $1,500 level you typically get three to five template-based pages, while $7,500–$15,000 buys discovery, strategy and several unique page layouts. For a single-location service business, the $3,000–$10,000 band is where most useful quotes will land.

Accessibility, privacy and tax rules in California

The Unruh Act makes accessibility a money question

California's Unruh Civil Rights Act incorporates the federal ADA, and it sets a minimum of $4,000 in statutory damages for each violation, as law firm Mintz explains. That minimum is a big reason California generates so many website accessibility claims. Mintz also notes an important limit: after the 2022 Martinez v. Cot'n Wash appeal ruling, businesses that exist only online are generally outside this route, because the law was tied to a physical place of public accommodation.

For a restaurant, clinic, salon or shop with premises in California, the practical advice is simple: ask your designer to build to the WCAG accessibility guidelines from day one. Descriptive image text, sufficient colour contrast, keyboard-friendly menus and properly labelled forms cost little during a build and a great deal after a demand letter.

The CCPA mostly affects larger businesses

The California Consumer Privacy Act applies to for-profit businesses with annual gross revenue above $26,625,000 (the threshold as adjusted from January 1, 2025), or that meet other tests based on buying, selling or sharing personal information. Most small businesses fall below those lines. A clear privacy policy and honest cookie settings are still good practice.

Web design isn't taxed, but software is about to be

Custom website design and development is not subject to California sales tax. What changes is the treatment of prewritten software: SB 122, signed on June 29, 2026, extends sales tax to prewritten software from January 1, 2027, including software accessed remotely, while custom software made for a single customer stays outside it. If your website relies on paid plugins, themes or subscription tools, expect some of those line items to carry tax from 2027. The design work itself should still be invoiced as a separate, custom service.

Running costs after launch

  • Maintenance: $100–$500 a month for updates, backups and small changes (Webment Digital).
  • Ongoing SEO: $500–$2,500 a month if you want continued search growth.
  • Platform fees: $27–$49 a month on Wix, around $33 on Squarespace and $45 on WordPress.com business plans.
  • E-commerce products: $25–$75 per product if someone else loads your catalogue.

In California especially, budget a line for an accessibility check before launch. It is far cheaper than fixing a live site under legal pressure.

How to compare California quotes

  1. Ask whether the build will follow WCAG accessibility guidelines, and which checks are run before launch.
  2. Ask for the page list and features in writing, with a fixed price.
  3. Check that paid plugins and themes are listed separately, since some will be taxable from 2027.
  4. Confirm the domain, hosting and analytics accounts are in your business's name.
  5. Ask what "SEO included" means: keyword research, structured data, Google Business Profile setup.
  6. Compare the total over two years, including maintenance, not just the launch price.

Hiring outside California

Bay Area and Los Angeles rates are hard to justify for a five-page site, which is why some Californian businesses look further afield. Working with a designer in India means a 12.5-hour difference in summer and 13.5 in winter. That works best asynchronously: you send notes in the evening and review changes the next morning. A short weekly call can be scheduled in your evening, which is the Indian morning.

The accessibility point above applies wherever your designer is. Make sure the contract names WCAG as the standard, and that you own the finished site. Our guide to hiring a web designer from India covers contracts, payments and vetting.
