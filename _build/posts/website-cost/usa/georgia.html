Agency tiers in Atlanta

Trajectory Web Design splits the Atlanta market into three tiers:

  • DIY and freelance ($500–$5,000): some excellent local freelancers, but hard to filter. Trajectory says many $1,500 quotes come from template shops marketing to Atlanta businesses, and recommends asking for live work and references.
  • Local small agency ($10,000–$40,000): senior teams, with competing studios usually quoting in a similar band for the same scope.
  • Specialised or large agency ($40,000–$150,000+): brand, content, UX research and complex development.

Trajectory's small-agency tier is higher than Bretz Media's small business range, which reflects the two firms' positioning. For a local service business, Bretz's $5,000–$15,000 band is the more common starting point.

What makes Atlanta pricing different

An above-average market

Bretz Media estimates Atlanta prices run 10–20% above the national average, because local demand is strong and developers serve a mix of funded start-ups and established businesses. Trajectory adds that Atlanta has a high density of web and marketing agencies, which keeps competition in the small-agency tier tight.

Hourly rates

Bretz gives $75–$200 an hour as the overall range: junior developers and offshore teams at around $50–$100, and senior developers and Atlanta-based agencies at $125–$200. Retainers for ongoing work usually begin near $500 a month, rising past $2,000 a month when development is continuous.

No sales tax on web design

According to the Georgia Department of Revenue, the state taxes tangible personal property and certain listed services, such as accommodations, some transportation and admission charges; most services are not taxed. Custom web design and development therefore shouldn't carry Georgia sales tax, though tangible items sold with a project can.

Privacy

Georgia has no comprehensive consumer privacy law in force, according to the IAPP's state tracker. A clear privacy policy and careful handling of form data remain good practice.

Add-ons and running costs

Atlanta quotes often exclude items that make a big difference to the result. Bretz Media's figures:

  • Copywriting: $100–$300 a page; photography $500–$2,000.
  • SEO foundation (keyword research, competitor analysis, citations, schema, content strategy): $1,500–$5,000.
  • Integrations such as booking, member portals, payments or CRM connections: $500–$5,000 each.
  • Extra revision rounds: $200–$500 each, beyond the two or three usually included.
  • Managed support: $100–$500 a month after any included post-launch period.
  • DIY platforms: $16–$65 a month, plus premium templates and $20–$100 a month in apps.

Is a bigger budget worth it?

Bretz Media frames the decision in terms of payback rather than price. Its example is a law firm in Buckhead: if each new client brings in $5,000, a $10,000 website has paid for itself after two new clients. For a business where each customer is worth less, the same logic points to a smaller build. It also puts a number on the DIY route: expect 20–60 hours learning a platform, building pages and troubleshooting, time that has a cost of its own. Work out what one new customer is worth to you before comparing quotes.

How to compare Atlanta quotes

  1. Ask whether the quote includes copywriting, photography and an SEO foundation, or whether those are extra.
  2. List the integrations you need and ask for each to be priced.
  3. Check how many revision rounds are included.
  4. Ask what post-launch support is included, and for how long.
  5. Confirm you will own the domain, hosting and site.
  6. Ask for live sites and references from businesses like yours.

Hiring outside Georgia

Georgia is on Eastern Time, so India is 9.5 hours ahead in summer and 10.5 in winter. With a designer in India, feedback goes over in your afternoon and changes come back overnight; early morning is the best time for a call.

Trajectory's warning about cheap template shops is fair for any market: the location of a designer tells you little, and live work tells you a lot.
