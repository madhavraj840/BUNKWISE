Quick answer

In 2026, a standard small business website in Canada typically costs C$3,500–C$5,000, with basic sites starting around C$1,500 and custom builds at C$6,000–C$10,000 or more, according to Canada Web Pro. Small agencies charge C$5,000–C$15,000 and full-service agencies C$20,000–C$80,000. On top of the build, GST or HST applies depending on your province. Canada's web design market spans budget studios selling C$599 packages and Toronto or Vancouver agencies whose projects start in the tens of thousands. What makes Canada different from the US is that the rules change at the provincial border: the sales tax on your invoice, whether you need a French version of the site, and whether accessibility standards are a legal requirement. This guide covers the prices first, then those provincial differences.

Whether provincial sales tax applies to web design services depends on each province's rules, so ask your accountant if the quote doesn't say. For a GST/HST-registered business, the tax on a website is normally recoverable as an input tax credit.

Quebec: the French version must be equal

If you sell to customers in Quebec, the Charter of the French Language, as amended by Bill 96, applies to your website. Law firm Smart & Biggar explains that commercial websites are treated as commercial documents, so the French version must be available on terms at least as favourable as any other language. A thin French summary next to a full English site does not meet that standard. For a Quebec business, this roughly doubles the content work, and translation alone is priced at C$0.20–C$0.35 a word by Click Web Studio.

Ontario: accessibility is a legal requirement for larger businesses

Under the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act, businesses and non-profits with 50 or more employees have had to make their public websites meet WCAG 2.0 Level AA since January 1, 2021. Smaller businesses are not covered by that deadline, but the same basics (image descriptions, colour contrast, keyboard navigation, labelled forms) make a site easier for everyone to use and cost little to build in from the start.

Running costs after launch

Canada Web Pro puts the typical monthly running costs at:

  • Hosting and SSL: C$20–C$60 a month
  • Maintenance and updates (optional): C$50–C$150 a month
  • Domain renewal: C$15–C$45 a year, for a .ca or .com

Elevate adds the costs people tend to forget: business email at C$6–C$12 per mailbox a month, a photography shoot at C$500–C$3,500, copywriting at C$300–C$2,500, and an SEO retainer at C$500–C$3,000 a month if you want ongoing search work.

How to compare Canadian quotes

  1. Check whether the quote is before or after GST/HST, and which province's rate applies.
  2. If you sell into Quebec, ask whether the French version is included and who translates it.
  3. Ask whether the site will be built to WCAG 2.0 AA, especially if you have 50+ staff in Ontario.
  4. Get the page list and features in writing, with a fixed price.
  5. Make sure the .ca domain is registered to your business.
  6. Ask what the monthly fee includes, and what happens to the site if you stop paying it. Some low-cost packages rent you the site rather than sell it.

Working with a designer outside Canada

India is 9.5 hours ahead of Toronto and Montreal in summer (10.5 in winter) and 12.5 hours ahead of Vancouver (13.5 in winter). In practice, feedback you send in the afternoon is worked on overnight, and live calls fit best in the early Eastern morning. Projects run mainly in writing, with a video call at each milestone.

The trade-offs are real: no in-person meetings, and a designer abroad needs you to explain your local market and competitors.
